# The candidates, and why each lost

| Candidate | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Assistant over the whole system of record** | **Winner** | Passes all five tests |
| Standalone service-department copilot | Folded into the winner | Weak moat on its own: a generic assistant product could reach the same service data through existing integrations. It survives as the winner's first workflow |
| Slip pricing and occupancy forecasting | Lost | Dockwa's Telescope already does this, with over ten years of booking data DockMaster cannot match. We would be attacking the competitor where they are strongest |
| Voice / AI sales automation | Lost | The BluSynq partnership already covers voice, and anyone could build the rest. We would be arguing against our own partnership |
| Exposing DockMaster's data to any AI tool (MCP/API as the product) | Lost as a product, kept as a feature | Very few marina customers run their own AI tools today, and handing the customer relationship to third-party assistants gives away the most valuable thing DockMaster has |

### The competitive picture behind this

Dockwa is turning its booking network into AI products (Telescope for pricing, Marina-X for underwriting). DockMaster cannot win a fight over booking data. But Dockwa does not have the operational record: work orders, labour, parts, invoices. The product an outsider cannot build sits on that record. And DockMaster has no public API today, so the tool layer this product needs is also the integration surface the company is missing. Build it once, and several things can use it.
