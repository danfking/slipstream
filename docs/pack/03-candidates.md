# The candidate field, and the kills

| Candidate | Verdict | The killing criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Cross-department ops assistant** over the system of record | **Winner** | Passes all five |
| Standalone service-department copilot | Folded into the winner | Small moat: replaceable by any generic assistant product that reaches the service data through existing integrations. Survives as the winner's anchor workflow |
| Slip/berth yield management (dynamic pricing) | Killed | Dockwa's Telescope already does this, on ten-plus years of reservation-network data DockMaster cannot match. Attacking the competitor's strongest ground from a weaker data position |
| Voice / AI-SDR workflow automation | Killed | The BluSynq partnership already fills the voice slot; any outsider could build the rest. A build-vs-partner argument we'd lose against our own existing arrangement |
| Naked MCP/API exposure of DockMaster capabilities | Rejected as *the product*, adopted as *a surface* | The buyer who can consume it barely exists in this base, and ceding the client to third-party assistants spends the moat rather than monetising it |

### The competitive shape that decided it

Dockwa is monetising its **reservation network** (Telescope pricing, Marina-X underwriting). DockMaster cannot win a network-data fight — but Dockwa doesn't have the **operational record**: work orders, labour, parts, invoices. The venture an outsider can't build sits on that record. Notably, DockMaster has **no public API today** — the tool layer this product needs is also the integration surface the company is missing. One build, several consumers.
