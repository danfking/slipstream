# Build vs partner: partner the channels, own the record

DockMaster's stated strategy is partner-with-best-in-class, and its strongest shipped AI (the Blu Voice Agent) is a partnership. So why build this?

### The dividing line

**Partner on commodity AI capability. Own the data and the leverage of it.** You use the commodity pieces to turn your rich knowledge into customer value. This product *is* the record made conversational: its entire worth is the semantics of DockMaster's own data. What a work order means, how labour becomes an invoice, why a haul-out blocks a slip. A partner building that acquires the crown-jewel knowledge on someone else's balance sheet, and the dependency runs the wrong way forever after.

### Build the integration surface

There is no public API today; integrations are a curated, point-to-point partner program. The tool layer this product requires is the missing API: infrastructure you would never outsource to an assistant vendor. Once it exists, the assistant on top is the thin part, and the same layer serves three consumers: the first-party assistant, partners like Blu, and (selectively) customers' own AI tooling.

### The group fit

The group already builds AI in-house and replays it: ventures incubated on one portfolio company's distribution, then sold beyond it. This product fits that machine exactly. Build it and the group owns a reusable asset; partner it and the group rents a dependency whose price can rise any year.

### Where partnering stays right

Voice stays with Blu; our layer should feed it context, not replace it. And a bounded partner pilot to prove demand while the tool layer is built would not be heresy. The line is the record, not a religion about building.
