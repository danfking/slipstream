# Build or partner?

DockMaster's stated strategy is to partner with best-in-class rather than build, and its strongest shipped AI (the Blu Voice Agent) is a partnership. So why build this one?

### The dividing line

Partner on commodity AI capability. Own the data and the leverage of it. The commodity pieces turn your rich knowledge into customer value, and this product is that knowledge: what a work order means, how labour becomes an invoice, why a haul-out blocks a slip. If a partner builds that, the most valuable knowledge in the company ends up on someone else's balance sheet, and DockMaster depends on them from then on.

### The tool layer is the missing API

DockMaster has no public API today. Integrations are individual arrangements with eight named partners. The tool layer this product needs is the API the company never built. That is core infrastructure, and you would not hand core infrastructure to an assistant vendor. Once it exists, the assistant on top is the smaller part of the work, and the same layer can serve the assistant, partners like Blu, and, where it makes sense, a customer's own AI tools.

### It fits how the group already works

The group builds AI in-house and reuses it: ventures launched on one portfolio company's customers, then sold beyond it. This product fits that approach. Build it and the group owns something it can use again. Partner it and the group rents something whose price can go up every year.

### Where partnering stays right

Voice stays with Blu. Our layer should feed Blu context, not compete with it. And if a partner could prove customer demand quickly while the tool layer is being built, a small time-limited pilot would be a reasonable thing to do. The dividing line is about who owns the record, and it cuts both ways.
